What the numbers show today

Hyperscalers cost about 1.8x more

The cheapest on-demand H100 at a hyperscaler is $6.88 per GPU-hour, against a market median of $3.92. Big clouds close the gap with reservations and savings plans.

The H200 premium is about 19%

At providers that offer both, the H200 costs on average about 19% more than the H100, while giving 76% more memory (141 GB vs 80 GB) and faster bandwidth. For large models it is often better value.

B200 costs about 2.0x an H100

The median B200 rents for $7.84 per hour, but offers much higher FP8 and FP4 throughput and 192 GB of memory per GPU.

A100 is the budget option

An A100 rents from $0.89 per hour. It lacks FP8, but is still strong for fine-tuning and inference of smaller models.

How to choose a GPU cloud

  • Match the GPU to the model. Check memory first with our guide to how much VRAM an LLM needs, then compare speed. See H100 vs H200 and B200 vs H100.
  • Look at the full bill. Storage, data transfer (egress) and idle time can add a lot to the GPU price.
  • Check availability. The cheapest listings are often out of stock or limited to single GPUs.
  • Multi-GPU training needs fast links. For training across many GPUs, choose SXM servers with NVLink and InfiniBand networking. Learn why in NVLink and NVSwitch explained.
  • Commit only when usage is steady. Reserved pricing saves money, but only if you will keep the GPUs busy.

Methodology and sources

Every day we compile on-demand list prices published by providers, as aggregated by public GPU price trackers. Prices are converted to US dollars per GPU per hour; multi-GPU instance prices are divided by the number of GPUs. The median is calculated across the providers listed for each GPU. We exclude spot, reserved and custom-quote pricing. Prices change frequently and may differ by region, so always confirm on the provider's site. Sources: GetDeploying GPU price data, GPU Per Hour price index and Thunder Compute H200 price comparison.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an H100 cost per hour?

As of November 23, 2026, the median on-demand price for an NVIDIA H100 across the 22 providers we track is $3.92 per GPU-hour. The lowest listing is $1.80 (HyperAI), and hyperscalers such as AWS, Azure, Oracle and Google Cloud charge about $6.88 to $11.07 per GPU-hour on demand.

How much does an H200 cost per hour?

The median on-demand rate is $4.92 per GPU-hour, with listings from $2.09 to $13.78. At providers that offer both, the H200 costs on average about 19% more per hour than the H100.

How much does a B200 cost per hour?

The median on-demand price is $7.84 per GPU-hour, ranging from $4.11 (Beam) to $14.24.

Why are AWS, Azure and Google Cloud more expensive?

Hyperscalers bundle GPUs with enterprise networking, security, compliance, support and integration with their other services, and they mostly rent full 8-GPU servers. Specialist GPU clouds focus on GPUs alone and compete hard on price. Hyperscaler prices drop a lot with reservations, savings plans or spot capacity.

What is the difference between on-demand, reserved and spot pricing?

On-demand means paying by the hour with no commitment, the prices shown here. Reserved or committed pricing gives large discounts for 1 to 3 year contracts. Spot or interruptible capacity is cheapest but can be taken back at short notice, so it suits jobs that can restart.

Is it cheaper to rent or buy a GPU?

Renting is cheaper unless you can keep GPUs busy most of the time for years. A data center GPU costs tens of thousands of dollars to buy, plus servers, power, cooling and networking. At the current median H100 price, a GPU rented around the clock costs about $2,862 per month, so buying only pays off with high, steady utilisation.

How often is this page updated?

Prices are refreshed every day and recorded in the price history table, so you can see how the market moves. Prices can change during the day, so always confirm the final price on the provider site before you rent.
